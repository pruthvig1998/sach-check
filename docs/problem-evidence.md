### 2.1 Internet users and language of use

- **Kantar and IAMAI, Internet in India 2024** (ICUBE 2024). Publisher: Internet and Mobile Association of India with Kantar. URL: https://www.iamai.in/sites/default/files/research/Kantar_%20IAMAI%20report_2024_.pdf
  - Total internet users chart ends at 886 Mn (2024); rural 488 Mn and urban 397 Mn.
  - Text on the language page: 870 million internet users (98% of internet users) accessed the internet in Indic languages in 2024, and 57% of internet users prefer Indic content in urban India.
  - Another chart in the same report gives "Indic users, all India: 842 million". The two numbers use different bases that the report does not reconcile on the pages read. Quote the 870 million / 98% sentence and say "accessed in Indic languages", not "only use Indic languages".
  - How verified: the PDF was downloaded and its text layer extracted; the numbers above were read in the extracted text. The Week/PTI and Business Today (16 Jan 2025) report the same 886 million, 98%, 57%, 488 million figures.

- **DataReportal, Digital 2026: India** (Kepios). URL: https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-india
  - About 1.03 billion internet users in October 2025 (70.0% of population), up about 223 million (+27.7%) from October 2024; about 440 million not using the internet. Social media user identities about 500 million.
  - The page does not mention WhatsApp. DataReportal itself warns that figures are modelled and that year-on-year comparisons across its reports can mislead.
  - Conflict to show: Kantar-IAMAI gives 886 million for 2024; DataReportal's change figure implies a much larger jump. Both are estimates by different methods. Do not compute a trend from the two.
  - Confidence: high that DataReportal published it; it is an estimate.
- **TRAI, monthly telecom subscription data, June 2026** (released 28 Jul 2026, PR No. 104 of 2026). URL: https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-07/PR_No104of2026_0.pdf

  - The TRAI PDF itself could not be parsed by the fetch tool (compressed), so this is a secondary report of the primary. One search result showed a different broadband figure (1,065.88 million), which looks like an earlier month; not used.
  - Subscriptions are connections, not unique people.
  - Confidence: medium.

### 2.4 Language structure (Census 2011)

- Census of India 2011, Paper 1 of 2018 (Language), table C-16. URL: https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/42458/download/46089/C-16_25062018.pdf

## 3. Exposure to misinformation

- **Reuters Institute 2025, India:** 53% of Indian respondents named Meta-owned WhatsApp as the channel carrying the biggest threat from false or misleading information, "the highest score across all markets" per the report. Same URL as section 2.3. Confidence: high.
- **Reuters Institute 2026 (search result, not read on the page):** a search summary attributed "58%" concern about fake news online to India against a 62% global average. We did not locate that sentence on the India page we read, so it is excluded.
- **Pew Research Center, 25 Mar 2019:** 77% of Indian adults were very or somewhat concerned about exposure to false information through mobile phones, 45% very concerned (from a 2018 survey; sample size and fieldwork dates not stated on the short read). URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2019/03/25/indian-elections-nearing-amid-frustration-with-politics-concerns-about-misinformation/. The same page gives 29% of Indian adults currently using WhatsApp (2018). That is an old figure from a face-to-face adult sample that includes people who are offline, so do not set it against current numbers. Confidence: high.
- **Pew, April 2025 (35 countries):** the report page gives a 35-country median of 59% calling made-up news a very big problem. India's own figure was in a chart we could not read from the PDF. Secondary sites say 65% and 81% for India; we could not confirm these. Excluded.
- **Microsoft Digital Civility Index, Feb 2019:** 64% of Indian respondents had encountered fake news (global average 57%), 22 countries; 54% internet hoaxes; 42% phishing or spoofing. Read on WION: https://www.wionews.com/india-news/india-topping-fake-news-menace-globally-may-increase-ahead-of-general-elections-microsoft-survey-195508 (published 6 Feb 2019). Business Standard/PTI carries the same story but returned 403. Sample size not stated. Confidence: medium. Older data.
- **LSE, WhatsApp Vigilantes (Banaji and Bhat, Oct 2019):** qualitative study; finds prejudice, not low digital literacy, drives spread of rumour that can trigger mob violence, and that many users forward messages from trusted contacts without verifying. The LSE news page did not state sample size or states; a search summary mentioned over 200 interviews in four states, not confirmed on the page. URL: https://www.lse.ac.uk/news/latest-news-from-lse/j-october-2019/prejudice-rather-than-digital-illiteracy-behind-the-spread-of-fake-news-on-whatsapp-in-india. Confidence: high for the findings stated; this is not a prevalence figure. Useful for the design argument (a trusted-contact forward is the norm) and as a caution that fact-checking alone does not remove prejudice.

## 4. Harm

### 4.1 Violence linked to rumours

- **IndiaSpend analysis, published 9 Jul 2018** (Saldanha, Rajput, Hazare), republished by BOOM. URL: https://www.boomlive.in/child-lifting-rumours-33-killed-in-69-mob-attacks-since-jan-2017-before-that-only-1-attack-in-2012
  - Period 1 Jan 2017 to 5 Jul 2018: 33 deaths, at least 99 injured, 69 reported attacks. As of 6 Jul 2018, 2018 alone had 24 deaths in 61 attacks; 2017 had 11 deaths in 8 attacks. One comparable attack was found for 2012.
  - Attribution: about 77% of the 69 cases were attributed to fake news spread on social media; WhatsApp was the named rumour source in 19 cases (28%).
  - Established: victims were attacked on suspicion, and no evidence of child-lifting was later found.
  - Not established: the counts come from English-language news reports and may miss cases; the article says NCRB data does not show a matching rise in child kidnappings. The share attributed to WhatsApp is from news reports, not independent verification.
  - Confidence: medium (a secondary republication of a primary data analysis).
- **Government record:** a search summary reported that the Indian Government does not track lynchings, and that the Home Ministry directed states on 5 Jul 2018 to contain rumour-driven lynchings. We did not read a primary government page for either point, so neither is used in the table. Do not state an official death toll.
- **LSE 2019** also says that, since 2014, over a hundred lynchings of members of discriminated communities occurred, many on cattle-related allegations. This is the LSE press page's claim and is not specific to WhatsApp; do not attribute it to WhatsApp.

Confidence: low and dated.
  - Legal status: the Bombay High Court tie-breaker judge, Justice Atul Chandurkar, struck down on 20 Sep 2024 the 2023 IT Rules amendment that enabled a government fact-check unit to direct takedowns of "fake news" about the Government (Newslaundry: https://www.newslaundry.com/2024/09/20/bombay-high-court-strikes-down-it-rules-amendment-by-which-govt-could-fact-check-news-on-itself). - **Independent fact-checkers**
  - The India Forum (Kanchan Kaur, 15 Jan 2025): India has the largest number of IFCN-certified fact-checkers of any country, 17 "at last count", noting some certificates may have lapsed; Indian partners in Meta's programme covered Hindi, Telugu, Bengali, Tamil and Malayalam. URL: https://www.theindiaforum.in/society/meta-pulling-plug-are-indian-fact-checkers-risk. Low (single secondary source). The IFCN's own signatory directory loaded blank to our fetch tool, so the count is unverified against the primary list.
  - The Misinformation Combat Alliance's Fact Checking Network of India is a separate body from the IFCN (search results only; not read).
- **Gap argument.** What the sources do and do not show:
  - Shown (high): the Reuters India sample is mainly English-speaking online users; Kantar-IAMAI says 98% of internet users accessed the internet in Indic languages in 2024; Census 2011 says 43.63% of people have Hindi and 96.71% a Scheduled language as mother tongue.

  - **Not shown:** we found no source giving the share of Indian fact-checks published in English versus regional languages, and no source that measures how many users cannot reach a fact-check in their language. The reasoning "English-only checking leaves most users unserved" is therefore an inference from the facts above, not a measured finding. State it as the project's argument, not as a statistic.
  - A 2021 arXiv paper on a fact-check dataset for Indian regional languages (FactDRIL, arXiv 2102.11276) reports 9,058 English, 5,155 Hindi and 8,222 other-language samples in its dataset, and justifies its focus by citing that only about 10% of the literate population speaks English. This was seen in a search summary only, concerns a research dataset not fact-check output, and was not opened. Excluded.

## 6. Claims we must NOT make

1. Any "X crore forwards a day" for India, or "WhatsApp India sees N messages daily". We found no primary source for an India-specific forward count.
2. "WhatsApp has 535 million (or 500 crore) users in India" as a current Meta figure. No official current number found; the 53 crore figure is a February 2021 ministerial statement and the "500 million" in TechCrunch is unattributed.
3. "Indians lost Rs 22,845 crore to fake news / fake forwards." The figure is all reported financial cyber fraud in 2024. No source we read attributes any share to forwards or fake links.
4. "WhatsApp rumours killed 33 people." The IndiaSpend count is 33 deaths in 69 attacks over child-lifting rumours from social media in general; WhatsApp was the named source in 19 cases, and the data are from news reports.
5. Any official national death toll from WhatsApp lynchings. We did not find one.
6. "56% of Indians get news on WhatsApp" or "46% of Indians". These are percentages of an online, mainly English-speaking survey sample, not of the population.
7. "India has the most fake news in the world." The Microsoft 2019 survey found 64% of its Indian respondents had encountered fake news; headlines claiming India tops the world are about that survey of 22 countries only.
8. Pew 2025 "65% of Indians say made-up news is a very big problem" and "81% at least moderately". Not confirmed on the report; do not use until checked against the Pew chart.
9. Any COVID-19 misinformation death or loss number for India.
10. "N Indian IFCN signatories" (17 is a low-confidence single report), or "fact-checkers cover all 22 languages".
11. That PIB Fact Check is the national fact-checker for viral messages in general, or that it publishes in all regional languages.
12. A "2025 national cyber-fraud loss", a 2022 loss figure, or "Rs 1.2 lakh crore projected". None was confirmed on a primary or twice-corroborated source.
13. Any statement that the figures are from the Census of 2021 or later; the language data are from 2011.
